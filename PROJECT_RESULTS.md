Without LendingClub's own rate and grade fields, test ROC-AUC is 0.6646, so those fields add about 0.020.
Class imbalance: 15.5% of training loans default. Handled with a validation-tuned F1 threshold, with class-weighted logistic regression as a comparison: test ROC-AUC 0.6802 unweighted vs 0.6805 weighted, and Brier 0.1225 vs 0.1738. Probabilities stay unweighted.
Leakage control: 25 inputs are all known at origination; 41 post-origination fields are barred and tests enforce it. Evidence: `configs/feature_policy.yaml`, `tests/test_model_leakage.py`.

**Why 0.82 was not reached:** only fields known at origination are allowed, and the model is scored on a later vintage than it was trained on. No tuning was done toward the target. A much higher score would be a reason to check for post-origination fields, which are leakage.

### Optimizations, benchmarked under comparable conditions

| Change | Before | After | Factor | Conditions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| CSV to Parquet on HDFS, group-by on grade | 1.56 s | 0.14 s | 10.8x | Median of 3 runs. Parquet holds validated rows and CSV holds all loan rows, a difference of about 0.2%. One small query, not a general speed-up. |
| Partial and date indexes in PostgreSQL, 3-month cohort query | 166 ms | 11 ms | 14.5x | Median of 5 runs, same session, no parallel workers. Selective query chosen to use the index. |

Storage: 1.68 GB CSV (all columns) vs 127 MB Parquet (selected and derived columns). These hold different columns, so this is not a pure compression ratio.
Spark vs pandas: Single runs, not identical scope. The Spark run also writes silver and quarantine Parquet to HDFS, derives features and builds gold tables. Spark 24.2 s, pandas 22.4 s. This does not show that Spark is faster for this data size.
